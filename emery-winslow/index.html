Why Emery Winslow

Hydrostatic weighing has been Emery Winslow's speciality for decades. What sets it apart, in the manufacturer's own terms:

  • No electronics at the scale

    Hydrostatic load cells turn weight into fluid pressure. Stainless tubing carries it to a totalizer in a clean, dry area; only there does it become an electronic signal.

  • Made for water, washdown, lightning and surges

    The manufacturer lists water, high-pressure washdown, lightning, power surges, rodents and RFI/EMI among the causes of failure its load cells are designed to withstand.

  • Stainless steel construction

    Stainless steel load cells (304, with 316 available for the 136 Series) and 316 stainless tubing for corrosive and sanitary areas.

  • Consistently accurate, longer service life

    Emery Winslow states consistently accurate weighing and a longer service life than electronic load cells in harsh sites.

  • Lower running costs over time

    Fewer emergency service calls, less downtime and fewer load cell replacements, according to the manufacturer.

  • Made in the USA, roots in 1868

    Roots in the A.H. Emery Company, founded in 1868; joined with Winslow (founded 1896) in 1974. Built at the Seymour, Connecticut manufacturing center.

Strengths as stated by Emery Winslow Scale Company: Hydrostatic Weighing Systems brochure (06-15-2026), 120 and 136 Series sheets, and emerywinslow.com/about.

Hydrostatic technology

Every Emery Winslow Scale system uses hydrostatic load cells: the load becomes fluid pressure, and the electronics sit in a clean room away from the scale.

Hydrostatic™ Technology

About the manufacturer

Emery Winslow Scale Company manufactures hydrostatic weighing systems in Seymour, Connecticut, USA. Its load cells contain no electronics: weight becomes fluid pressure, stainless tubing carries it to a totalizer in a clean area, and only there is it converted into an electronic signal for the weight indicator.

HearS offers Emery Winslow systems to industrial customers in Vietnam, with selection, project engineering and installation support from our team. The solutions on these pages are the ones Emery Winslow asked HearS to focus on.

Why sites switch to hydrostatic

  • No electronic load cells beneath the scale
  • Weighing by fluid pressure
  • No electronics at the scale, so water, lightning and power surges have little to damage
  • Stainless steel construction for severe washdown
  • Fewer emergency service calls and less downtime, according to the manufacturer

What one failing load cell costs

Emery Winslow illustrates the hidden cost of electronic scales with a worked example: a poultry processing plant with 4 load cell failures a year, USD 2,500 repair cost and 8 hours of downtime each, and a production impact of USD 1,500 per hour.

That is USD 10,000 in repairs plus USD 48,000 in downtime: USD 58,000 a year. The figures are the manufacturer's illustration, not a HearS measurement; we can run the same sum with your own numbers.

  • Industries the manufacturer names: meat, poultry, seafood, dairy and food processing
  • Chemical, solvent and corrosive washdown environments
  • Aggregate, recycling, asphalt, mining and steel
  • Pharmaceutical and sanitary production; 24/7 operations
